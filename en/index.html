Registration and testing process

The steps below are a proposed framework. NETC must check them against the process in use before publication.

Six steps from request to test report

  1. Submit a test request. State the vehicle or engine type, the standard to test against and the number of samples. Request form
  2. Receive scope confirmation and a quotation. NETC confirms the applicable tests and sends a quotation. Response time
  3. Sign the contract. Contract template, confidentiality terms
  4. Deliver the sample. Deliver the vehicle or engine to the center with the technical documents. Documents, acceptance conditions
  5. Testing. Carried out in the relevant test laboratory. Time for each test
  6. Receive the test report and pay. Collect the report at the report reception and release desk; fees are collected and invoices issued at the fee collection desk.

About NETC

General introductionStructure and contactCapabilities and accreditation

What the center does

NETC is a public service unit under the Vietnam Register, Ministry of Construction, with legal status and independent accounting. The center was established on 21 Sep 2009 under Decision No. 2737/QĐ-BGTVT of the Minister of Transport. Its current functions and duties follow Decision 2292/QĐ-BXD of 12 Dec 2025.

Main duties

  • Provide testing services for emissions, fuel consumption and energy consumption of vehicles and equipment, and issue test reports.
  • Take part in drafting regulations, standards and related legal documents.
  • Witness tests and assess the technical conditions of other test facilities.
  • Build and operate a vehicle and equipment database.
  • Scientific research and technology transfer; prepare materials and assess emission inspectors.
  • Cooperate and form joint ventures and partnerships in Vietnam and abroad.

English summaries compiled by NETC from the original documents of regulators and international bodies. Each item gives its source and the original title; links lead to the original. Items marked TO BE CHECKED must be reviewed before publication. NETC's own news: TO BE ADDED.

Emission and energy regulations

EuropeApplies from 29 Nov 2026Source: EUR-Lex, the European Union's legal portal

Regulation (EU) 2024/1257 (Euro 7) on type-approval of motor vehicles and engines

Published in the Official Journal of the European Union on 8 May 2024. Applies to new M1 and N1 types from 29 Nov 2026 and to all new M1 and N1 vehicles from 29 Nov 2027; trucks and buses (M2, M3, N2, N3) from 29 May 2028 for new types and 29 May 2029 for all new vehicles. What is new: emissions must be met over the whole vehicle life, on-board emissions monitoring (NOx, particles, NH3), battery ageing monitoring for vehicles with traction batteries, tyre abrasion brought into scope (C1 tyres from 1 Jul 2028); the brake particle limit will be set later by a delegated act.

Original title: Regulation (EU) 2024/1257 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 24 April 2024 on type-approval of motor vehicles and engines and of systems, components and separate technical units

United StatesProposed 18 May 2026Source: Federal Register, the US official gazette

EPA proposes delaying the Tier 4 schedule for light- and medium-duty vehicles

The US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) proposes keeping Tier 3 for model years 2027 and 2028 and returning to Tier 4 from model year 2029. The fleet-average NMOG+NOx for light-duty vehicles is 30 mg/mile in 2027 and 2028, then 21 mg/mile in 2029. Comments were due by 6 Jul 2026. This is a proposal and was not in force at the time of the document; TO BE CHECKED: outcome after public comment.

Original title: Revision of Tier 4 Criteria Pollutant Standards, Part 1: Amendments to Phase-In Schedule for Light-Duty and Medium-Duty Vehicles (91 FR 28463)

ChinaFrom 07/2023Source: DieselNet (a compilation site, not a Chinese government body)

GB 18352.6-2016 (China 6): stages 6a and 6b and real-driving emissions measurement

China 6a applies nationwide from Jan 2021 and China 6b from Jul 2023. Real-driving emissions (RDE) testing is mandatory from Jul 2023 with a conformity factor of 2.1 for NOx and particles, excluding the cold-start phase. Durability is 200,000 km under China 6b. The standard was amended in 2025 on reporting defects in warranted components and on vehicles with three or more axles.

Original title: GB 18352.6-2016, as compiled by DieselNet: China: Cars and Light Trucks - Emission Standards

JapanTarget for fiscal year 2030Source: ICCT, presenting an MLIT document

Japan's fuel economy standard for passenger vehicles

The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) and the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) set a fiscal 2030 target of 25.4 km/L for passenger vehicles, 32.4% above actual fiscal 2016 performance, applied to manufacturers under the Act on the Rational Use of Energy. The document dates from 2021, TO BE CHECKED: latest MLIT update.

Original title: Overview of MLIT’s Vehicle Environmental Policy (document circulated by ICCT, 2021)

GlobalGlobal technical regulationSource: UNECE

UN GTR No. 22: in-vehicle battery durability

A global technical regulation of the UN Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE) that sets a method to assess the durability of traction batteries on the vehicle, complementing energy consumption and range testing.

Original title: United Nations Global Technical Regulation No. 22

United StatesTest facilitySource: EPA

EPA's National Vehicle and Fuel Emissions Laboratory (NVFEL)

EPA's main facility for testing vehicle emissions, developing standards and determining whether vehicles comply with federal emission and fuel economy standards. The laboratory is accredited to ISO 17025:2017 and certified to ISO 14001:2015.

Original title: National Vehicle and Fuel Emissions Laboratory (NVFEL)

New testing technologies to watch

  • Real-driving emissions (RDE). Already mandatory in China since 07/2023. Requires portable emissions measurement equipment fitted to the vehicle. Source: DieselNet.
  • On-board monitoring (OBM). Euro 7 requires vehicles to monitor NOx, particles and NH3 themselves. Source: EUR-Lex.
  • Battery ageing and durability. Euro 7 requires battery ageing to be monitored; UN GTR 22 sets a durability assessment method. Source: EUR-Lex, UNECE.
  • Brake particles and tyre abrasion. Europe is bringing them into scope; the brake particle limit will be set later by a delegated act. Source: EUR-Lex.
